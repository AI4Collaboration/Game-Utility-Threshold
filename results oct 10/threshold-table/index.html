Definitions, statistical method and limitations

What the thresholds mean

R = 1 and P = 0. At each sampled S–T point, for each decision’s reported opponent-cooperation belief q, EU(C) − EU(D) = q(1 − T) + (1 − q)S. Positive means C is the selfish expected-utility best response; negative means D; ties allow either. We recompute this using the belief reported at each point, rather than holding q at 0.5. These are conditional best responses, not equilibrium predictions or evidence of calibrated beliefs.

The observed threshold brackets a sampled C-majority → D-majority crossing as T increases, or a D-majority → C-majority crossing as S increases. The theoretical threshold uses the same crossing rule on the admissible best-response C-rate: strictly more than half must favor one action. Tied cells widen the bracket. Multiple/reversed crossings are retained rather than forced into one threshold. One-sided bounds require extending the single-crossing pattern. All brackets describe samples; no interpolation, binary search, logistic fit, or finite statistical precision is implied.

The signed grid difference is [observed lower − theoretical upper, observed upper − theoretical lower]. The minimum signed gap is its endpoint nearest zero when separated, zero when overlapping/touching, and unavailable when not located. Positive means a higher observed payoff threshold. For T that suggests more persistent cooperation; for S it suggests cooperation requires a higher S. Grid separation alone does not establish statistical significance.

95% confidence sets: method and limits

Each complete game supplies three bounded [0,1] scores: its two-player mean C choice, mean strict-C best response, and mean admissible-C best response (including ties). We use two-sided Hoeffding bands with radius √[log(2K/0.05)/(2n)], where n is complete games at that cell and K = 3 × 808 = 2,424 cell/quantity comparisons. Clipping gives [0,1]. The union bound provides at least 95% simultaneous coverage for all three mean-rate processes across the table, assuming independent games. It does not assume independence between players in a game, between the three quantities, or between slices.

There are only one or two games per cell. Every observed-rate band is [0,1] under this deliberately conservative method. We intersect theoretical-rate bands with exact payoff restrictions: strict D dominance predicts zero C for every belief, and strict C dominance predicts C for every belief. Weak dominance restricts the corresponding admissible endpoint. Thus some theoretical threshold sets retain one-sided information.

Inverting the observed-rate bands still permits every threshold location, including outside the sampled domain, conditional on a single monotone population crossing existing. Every observed threshold set and difference set is (−∞,+∞); the data also do not establish that a unique crossing exists. The “inside/outside” comparison is marked unresolved, and distance outside is unavailable—not zero and not evidence of agreement. Other methods could be narrower by imposing extra response-curve or independence assumptions.

The target is the mean response of the tested label blocks conditional on valid complete outputs. Two cells retain only one block; selection from invalid outputs and provider/time changes are not covered by these intervals. Refinement was collected later, with new seeds. Beliefs and choices are jointly reported, so this is descriptive consistency analysis, not a causal explanation. Theoretical sampling uncertainty is included; uncertainty in the reported beliefs’ faithfulness is not.
