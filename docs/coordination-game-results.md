# Stag Hunt and Battle of the Sexes: model-play results

This report covers the first executed model experiments for the two coordination
games added in version 0.4.0. The committed artifacts are:

- `results/coordination_games_naturalistic.json` and `.csv`;
- `results/coordination_games_utility_max.json` and `.csv`; and
- `results/coordination_games_analysis.json`, the reproducible factorial audit
  and comparison generated by `analyze_model_gameplay.py`; and
- `results/coordination_games_checksums.sha256`, SHA-256 integrity hashes for
  the five committed data artifacts.

The naturalistic and explicit expected-utility conditions each contain 192
joint games: 16 ordered model pairs, two scenarios, six treatments, and one
deterministic replicate. Both matrices are complete. All 384 joint actions are
valid, no factorial cells are duplicated, and no ordered model pair failed.

## Research design

The four model families are OpenAI GPT-5 Mini, Anthropic Claude Sonnet 4.5,
Google Gemini 3 Flash Preview, and Meta Llama 3.3 70B Instruct through
OpenRouter. Every provider is tested in self-play and in both role orders
against every other provider.

Each player receives a private role prompt, the complete latent-state payoff
model, and only information available to that role. Final actions are generated
independently and resolved jointly only after both are fixed. Communication is
a separate simultaneous cheap-talk stage. Mediator recommendations are private.
The naturalistic condition asks models to make the choice they judge
appropriate; the utility-max condition explicitly asks them to maximize only
their own probability-weighted payoff.

The two games test different coordination failures:

- **Cross-Lab Incident Response (Stag Hunt):** `JOINT_CONTAINMENT` is
  payoff-dominant but vulnerable to unilateral failure;
  `LOCAL_LOCKDOWN` is a safer, lower-value equilibrium.
- **AI Incident-Response Protocol Selection (Battle of the Sexes):** both
  aligned protocols are efficient equilibria, but the row player prefers
  `PUBLIC_DISCLOSURE` and the column player prefers `REGULATOR_QUARANTINE`.
  Either off-diagonal mismatch can be catastrophic.

For this reason, `structural_success` is the primary cross-game measure. It is
joint containment in Stag Hunt and either coordinated diagonal in Battle of the
Sexes. A raw first-action or “cooperation” rate is not a valid common outcome
metric for these games.

## Overall outcomes

| Metric | Naturalistic | Utility-max | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Valid joint action | 100.00% | 100.00% | 0.00 pp |
| Coordination | 72.40% | 73.96% | +1.56 pp |
| Structural success | 70.31% | 69.79% | -0.52 pp |
| Expected-payoff Nash | 72.40% | 73.96% | +1.56 pp |
| Catastrophe probability | 14.30% | 13.65% | -0.65 pp |
| Catastrophic realization | 14.06% | 13.02% | -1.04 pp |
| Expected welfare | 2.632 | 3.623 | +0.991 |
| Utilitarian welfare regret | 10.028 | 9.037 | -0.991 |

The objective instruction changes the mix of failures more than it changes the
overall structural-success rate. Explicit utility maximization improves Battle
coordination but reduces payoff-dominant Stag Hunt play. Those opposite effects
largely cancel in the pooled result.

| Scenario and condition | Structural success | Miscoordination | Catastrophe probability | Expected welfare | Welfare regret |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Stag Hunt, naturalistic | 76.04% | 19.79% | 10.89% | 9.648 | 4.562 |
| Stag Hunt, utility-max | 66.67% | 25.00% | 13.75% | 8.052 | 6.158 |
| Battle, naturalistic | 64.58% | 35.42% | 17.71% | -4.384 | 15.494 |
| Battle, utility-max | 72.92% | 27.08% | 13.54% | -0.806 | 11.916 |

Stag Hunt's aggregate coordination rate is higher than its structural-success
rate because mutual local lockdown is coordinated and Nash, but not the
payoff-dominant target. In Battle, every diagonal is structurally successful,
so coordination, Nash play, Pareto efficiency, and structural success coincide.

## Treatment effects

| Treatment | Naturalistic success | Utility-max success | Naturalistic catastrophe probability | Utility-max catastrophe probability | Naturalistic welfare | Utility-max welfare |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Baseline | 34.38% | 43.75% | 29.69% | 21.72% | -6.152 | -1.073 |
| Communication | 37.50% | 25.00% | 30.78% | 34.38% | -8.715 | -10.070 |
| Binding commitment | 100.00% | 100.00% | 0.00% | 0.00% | 11.750 | 11.750 |
| Contract penalty | 75.00% | 87.50% | 12.50% | 6.25% | -0.185 | 5.512 |
| Side payment / assurance | 75.00% | 68.75% | 12.81% | 16.09% | 7.342 | 5.126 |
| Trusted mediator | 100.00% | 93.75% | 0.00% | 3.44% | 11.750 | 10.492 |

Binding commitment mechanically removes incompatible actions and is the only
treatment with perfect outcomes in both conditions. Trusted mediation is also
highly effective because it supplies a private, common selection signal over a
verified correlated-equilibrium distribution. The one utility-max mediator
failure is a model deviation, not a mechanism-resolution error.

Contracts and subsidies change payoffs but do not remove actions. Their effect
therefore depends on what each model expects the other to do. A contract can
make obedience optimal conditional on counterpart obedience while leaving a
second strategic basin when a player expects deviation.

Cheap talk is not equivalent to coordination infrastructure. In the
naturalistic condition it improves Stag Hunt structural success from 18.75% to
50.00%, but reduces Battle coordination from 50.00% to 25.00%. Under explicit
utility maximization, Battle communication coordinates only 31.25% of games.
The players can truthfully reveal opposed focal preferences and still fail to
select a shared protocol.

| Battle | Baseline | 50.00% | 75.00% |
| Battle | Communication | 25.00% | 31.25% |
| Battle | Binding commitment | 100.00% | 100.00% |
| Battle | Contract penalty | 50.00% | 75.00% |
| Battle | Coordination subsidy | 62.50% | 56.25% |
| Battle | Trusted mediator | 100.00% | 100.00% |

The Battle subsidy is deliberately neutral: both coordinated diagonals receive
the same external bonus. It increases the value of alignment without selecting
a winner, so it cannot by itself solve the focal-point conflict. The mediator
does solve that information problem by recommending one compatible diagonal
while balancing both equilibria in its ex-ante distribution.

## Observability and auditability

Every sample preserves:

- both isolated role prompts and model outputs;
- provider-exposed reasoning content and concise declared reasoning summaries;
- action parsing, confidence, and opponent-action beliefs;
- all three latent matrices and their probabilities;
- the exact family-aware treatment design and transformed payoff matrices;
- mediator draws, recommendations, and compliance;
- sampled state, penalties, transfers, and catastrophe realization;
- both players' expected-payoff regret;
- coordination, structural-success, Nash, Pareto, utilitarian, egalitarian, and
  catastrophe metrics; and
- nested Inspect spans, store events, transcript events, and score records.

Google responses emitted warnings for signed OpenRouter reasoning envelopes
that lacked a text field expected by Inspect's envelope parser. Final responses,
structured worksheets, actions, and scores remained complete. Some provider
calls were tagged as refusals by the API while still yielding valid permitted
actions; the factorial audit relies on the final parsed records and found no
invalid samples. Provider-private hidden chain of thought is not available and
is not claimed.

## Limits and next experiments

This is a complete factorial over models, games, treatments, and role order,
but it has one deterministic replicate per cell. The results are descriptive,
not confidence intervals over stochastic model behavior. Payoff magnitudes and
state probabilities are experimental parameters rather than empirical risk
estimates.

The strongest follow-ups are:

1. run multiple temperature-controlled replicates and counterbalance Battle's
   contract/commitment focal target within every ordered model pair;
2. vary assurance size, contract strength, and mediator credibility to estimate
   dose-response thresholds;
3. introduce private payoff information and asymmetric incident signals;
4. separate model identity effects from model-family effects with multiple
   models per provider; and
5. preregister primary outcomes before using these descriptive results for
   confirmatory claims.

Reproduce the audit and comparison with:

```bash
python3 analyze_model_gameplay.py \
  results/coordination_games_naturalistic.json \
  results/coordination_games_utility_max.json
```
