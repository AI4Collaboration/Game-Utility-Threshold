Units, ties and the familiar (5, 3, 1, 0) example

The baseline uses R = 1 and P = 0. Starting from raw payoffs (T, R, P, S) = (5, 3, 1, 0), subtract 1 and divide by 2 to obtain (2, 1, 0, −0.5), the Figure 3A preset. A raw defection penalty k therefore corresponds to z = k/2, or k = 2z. This change of units preserves mathematical payoff comparisons; it does not establish that a language model responds identically to the rewritten numbers.

At either calculated threshold, the two actions tie against that particular opponent action. At or above both thresholds, cooperation is at least as good against either action; strictly above both, it is strictly better. A tie is not a requirement to choose cooperation. Before intervention, strict Prisoner's Dilemma has T > 1 and S < 0, so defection is better against either action throughout that region.

The baseline can remain a strict Prisoner's Dilemma even when T + S > 2. In that case an unequal outcome gives more combined points than mutual cooperation. The action ordering alone does not establish that mutual cooperation maximizes total points. The penalty can also change the game's decision structure, as reported above.
