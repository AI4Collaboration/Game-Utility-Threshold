The payoff rules, assumptions, and connection to Paper 1

Agent incentives

Accurate + accepted: +1. Accurate + returned: 0. Inflated + accepted: +4. Inflated + returned: −penalty.

With k of 8 records checked, modeled detection is q = k/8. Expected inflation payoff is 4(1 − q) − penalty × q. Accurate reporting earns 1 under the clean-audit acceptance rule. They tie at q = 3/(4 + penalty). At a tie, both actions maximize points; the payoff-maximizing baseline chooses accurate.

Your decisions and cost

Accept an accurate report: +3. Return an inflated report: +2. Accept an inflated report: −6. Return an accurate report: −2. Subtract 0.25 for every record reviewed.

The reviewer mechanically compares numbers. It cannot miss an inspected mismatch or raise a false alarm. You can disagree with it. Research-agent and reviewer behavior are simulated; no model is trained. All scores are invented for this demonstration.

What this illustrates

Review budget → checked evidence → verdict → payoff. For Paper 1, a real study would measure reviewer errors and costs instead of assuming this detection rate. Our existing defense slider is a separate deterministic game.
