Study 01 / new LLM experiment

Collected 9 November 2026

Human–AI oversight: when defense makes cooperation pay.

A human defense is fixed before the model chooses cooperation or attack. We varied only defense, kept the payoff settings fixed, and compared a neutral numerical task with an explicit oversight story.

3,660Main decisions · 3 models
11 levelsDefense from 0% to 100%
$0.67Total API cost · includes 12 pilot calls

The models followed the higher payoff everywhere we sampled except at 30% defense, just above the mathematical crossing.

At that setting, cooperation pays 1 point and attack pays 0.9. Wording and display order affected some models’ choices despite identical payoffs.

GPT-4.1 mini selected the first displayed payoff row in all 40 oversight-story trials at 30% defense. Haiku’s neutral results also varied across presentation blocks. These patterns do not establish the models’ internal reasoning.

Design, source records, and limits

Each main defense setting has 100 fresh requests per model, balanced over four combinations of action labels and payoff-row order. The separate story check uses 40 requests at 0%, 30%, and 100% defense. The protocol was committed before collection. All 3,672 main and pilot requests returned valid actions; no retries or replacement observations were added.

Models saw computed exact payoffs. This tests responses to fixed defense choices, not empirical Nash or Stackelberg play, repeated learning, or real-world safety. The 12 compatibility pilot requests are retained separately and excluded from the main estimates.

Study 02 / simultaneous two-player games

Historical collection · 9 October 2026

PD, Stag Hunt, and Chicken across a payoff grid.

The exploratory dataset behind the original utility-threshold post. Four models played independent simultaneous games across a 13 × 13 grid, with mutual-cooperation payoff R = 1 and mutual-defection payoff P = 0.

169 settingsS and T varied · 4 models
1,350 / 1,352Paired games with two valid choices
99.77%Dominant-action agreement · 1,277 / 1,280

When one action paid more against either opponent choice, models chose it in 1,277 of 1,280 decisions.

Cooperation varied more in Chicken and Stag Hunt, where the better action depends on the other player. A cooperation rate alone cannot establish whether those choices were strategically correct.

A different experiment, kept separate

These observations vary the baseline S and T payoffs. The defection cost stayed at zero. The report’s separate figures about increasing defection cost are mathematical calculations, not additional model trials.

At most two paired games per model and payoff setting. These are exploratory observations, with no precise behavioral threshold or general model ranking established.

Source records, missing choices, and reproducibility

All 2,704 scheduled decisions are retained. Two answers were invalid; paired analysis also excludes their two valid partners, leaving 2,700 choices in 1,350 complete games. The source loader verifies hashes, manifests, prompts, role-specific payoffs, and action mappings before recomputing outcomes.

Recorded historical model identities are GLM-5.3, Qwen3.8, GPT-6 Astra, and Claude Fable 5.1. Their recorded settings and serving details are preserved; this is not a claim about current availability or relative model quality.
